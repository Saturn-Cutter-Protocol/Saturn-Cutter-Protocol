# Artifact — Iterative Human–AI Co-Adaptation

## Date

2026-08-30

## Status

Observation / Emerging Methodological Concept

Not yet claimed as a universally validated model of AI self-improvement.

## Title

Iterative Human–AI Co-Adaptation

## Observation

Longitudinal H-ACS work does not consist of isolated AI sessions. Outputs from earlier sessions are externally preserved, reviewed, corrected, and subsequently used as context for later sessions.

The interaction therefore forms a recursive feedback loop:

```text
SESSION
↓
OUTPUT
↓
EXTERNAL FIXATION
↓
REVIEW / AUDIT
↓
CORRECTION
↓
UPDATED CONTEXT / RULES
↓
NEXT SESSION
↓
NEW OUTPUT
```

The human operator remains the source of goals, context, selection, interpretation, and final agency. AI systems contribute generation, analysis, comparison, reduction, and audit functions.

## Finding

The observed process is best described as **iterative human–AI co-adaptation** or **recursive improvement of the human–AI interaction loop**.

The system changes over time because both sides of the interaction affect the next iteration: the human modifies questions, constraints, role definitions, and evidence requirements; subsequent AI responses incorporate that accumulated context and produce new material that can again be reviewed and incorporated.

This is a recursive improvement process at the level of the **human–AI interaction system**, not a claim that the underlying AI model autonomously rewrites or retrains itself.

## Engineering Interpretation

The distinction is important:

```text
Recursive Self-Improving AI Model

MODEL
↓
SELF-MODIFICATION / SELF-TRAINING
↓
NEW MODEL
```

is technically different from:

```text
Human–AI Interaction Loop

HUMAN + AI
↓
OUTPUT
↓
MEMORY / AUDIT
↓
CHANGED CONTEXT / RULES
↓
NEXT HUMAN + AI INTERACTION
```

H-ACS currently belongs to the second category.

## Operational Example

A mechanism or rule identified during one session can be classified, tested in subsequent real-world situations, reviewed later, and then used to change how the next session is conducted.

Example:

```text
Observation
↓
Hypothesis
↓
Working Rule
↓
Real-world application
↓
Observed result
↓
Audit
↓
Rule revision
↓
Next application
```

The SATURN Switch development process provides one concrete example of this broader interaction pattern.

## Core Principle

> **The interaction loop improves when the outputs of previous human–AI work become structured inputs to later human–AI work, while final agency remains human.**

## Boundaries

This artifact does not establish:

- that H-ACS constitutes recursive self-improvement in the technical AI/ML sense;
- that the AI model itself changes its underlying weights or architecture through these sessions;
- that iterative interaction necessarily produces improvement;
- that the present interaction architecture is optimal for other users.

Improvement must still be demonstrated through observable consequences and longitudinal comparison.

## Evidence Status

**Observation:** established from the documented longitudinal interaction process.

**Hypothesis:** the recursive interaction structure can systematically improve the quality, precision, and operational usefulness of subsequent human–AI work.

**Operationally Tested:** not yet assigned to the concept itself as an independent measured construct.

**Validated:** not established.

**Externalized:** intentionally deferred as a formal research stage.

## Research Significance

This concept provides a precise name for a central property of the H-ACS working environment: the system does not merely use AI repeatedly; it uses the record of previous human–AI interaction to modify the conditions of subsequent interaction.

The relevant object of study is therefore not only the human or the model, but the **evolving interaction loop between them**.

## Related H-ACS Structures

The concept is supported by existing H-ACS components:

- Cycle Table — longitudinal operational record;
- External Fixation — preservation of decisions and observations outside the live conversation;
- Artifacts — conversion of observations into durable objects;
- Audit — retrospective quality and evidence review;
- Repository — persistent methodological memory;
- Evidence Status Model — separation of observation, hypothesis, working rule, testing, validation, and externalization.

## Conclusion

**Iterative human–AI co-adaptation** is adopted as the current working description of the recursive improvement process observed across H-ACS sessions.

The term is preferable to **Recursive Self-Improvement** when describing the present system because it preserves the critical distinction between improvement of an interaction architecture and autonomous modification of an AI model itself.
